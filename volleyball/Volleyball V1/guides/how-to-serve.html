How to Serve in Volleyball: Types of Serve and Where to Aim

By the Spike Rally team · Updated 6 November 2026 · 7 min read

The serve is the only skill in volleyball you perform completely alone, with nobody else touching the ball first. That makes it the easiest skill to practise and one of the most valuable: every missed serve is a free point for the other team, and every tough serve makes their attack harder.

The underhand serve

Almost everyone starts here. It is easy to control and fine for recreational games.

  1. Stand behind the end line facing the net, with your non-hitting foot forward.
  2. Hold the ball in your non-hitting hand at waist height, in front of your hitting hip.
  3. Swing your hitting arm back, then forward like a pendulum, and strike the bottom-back of the ball with the heel of your hand or a closed fist.
  4. Shift your weight onto the front foot as you hit, and follow through towards your target.

Some players drop the ball slightly before hitting; others hit it straight out of the hand. Either is legal as long as you clearly release or strike it.

The overhand float serve

The float serve is the most common serve in the indoor game. Hit with no spin, the ball wobbles and moves unpredictably in the air, like a knuckleball in baseball.

  1. Stand with your non-hitting foot forward and your hitting arm already up, elbow high and hand by your ear.
  2. Toss the ball about half a metre to a metre above your hitting shoulder and slightly in front. The toss should barely spin. A bad toss is the cause of most bad serves.
  3. Step forward with your front foot as the ball rises.
  4. Hit the centre of the ball with a firm, flat palm and a stiff wrist. Stop your hand shortly after contact, rather than following through, so you do not add spin.

The topspin serve

The topspin serve is hit with a higher toss and a full arm swing, much like a spike. The heavy forward spin makes the ball dive sharply, so you can serve it hard and still keep it in the court. The toss goes higher and a little further in front, you contact the ball high, and you snap your wrist over the top of the ball with a full follow-through.

Jump serves

Advanced players add an approach and a jump to either serve. The jump float is a float serve hit at the top of a short jump: it is fairly safe and very hard to pass. The jump topspin serve uses a full spike approach and is the most powerful serve in the game, but also the riskiest. Remember that you may jump from behind the end line and land inside the court; you just cannot touch the line or the court before you hit the ball.

Where to aim

A serve that lands in but goes straight to the best passer is not much of a weapon. Aim with a purpose:

  • The seams: the gap between two passers. Both may go for it, or both may leave it.
  • The weakest passer: every team has one. Find them in the first few rallies.
  • The hitter who will attack next: making an attacker pass forces them to pass and then approach quickly, which tires them and limits their attack.
  • Deep corners: zones 1 and 5 make the passer move and reach.
  • Short: a soft serve just over the net in zone 2 or 4 can catch a team that stands deep. Use it as a surprise.
  • Behind the setter: serving to zone 1 when the setter is starting from right back forces them to cross the path of the pass.

Balancing power and risk

There is a constant trade-off between serving tough and serving in. A good guideline at amateur level is to serve at a level you can keep in about nine times out of ten, and to save your riskiest serve for when you are ahead. Never miss the serve right after a time-out or right after your team has just missed one; those are the moments to be safe and steady.

Build a routine

Most top servers use the same routine every time: the same number of bounces, the same breath, the same spot behind the line. A routine calms nerves and makes your toss consistent. Pick one and keep it.

Serving drills

  • Toss only: toss and let the ball drop. It should land just in front of your front foot every time.
  • Target zones: place towels in zones 1, 5 and 6 and keep score of how many serves hit or land near each.
  • Pressure serving: you must make five serves in a row before you can stop. Miss one and the count restarts.
