Beach Volleyball vs Indoor Volleyball: The Key Differences

By the Spike Rally team · Updated 6 November 2026 · 6 min read

Beach and indoor volleyball share a net, a ball and the three-touch rule, but they are really two different sports. If you have played one and want to try the other, these are the differences that will catch you out.

Two branches of one sport

Volleyball was invented in 1895 by William G. Morgan, a physical education teacher in Holyoke, Massachusetts, who wanted a gentler indoor game than basketball. He called it "Mintonette". Beach volleyball grew up on the beaches of California in the 1920s and 1930s and became a sport of its own. Indoor volleyball has been in the Olympic Games since Tokyo 1964; beach volleyball joined at Atlanta 1996.

Team size

Indoor teams have six players on court, with substitutions and a libero. Beach teams have two players and no substitutes at all. With two people covering the whole court, every player has to pass, set, attack, block and dig. There are no specialists.

The smaller court is necessary because two players cannot cover an indoor-sized court. Sand also slows movement and lowers the jump, so even strong indoor athletes find their first beach games exhausting.

Scoring

  • Indoor: sets to 25, best of five, deciding set to 15.
  • Beach: sets to 21, best of three, deciding set to 15. Every set is won by two.
  • On the beach, teams switch sides every seven points (every five in the third set), so that wind and sun affect both sides equally.

The block counts as a touch

Indoors, a block touch is a free contact: the team still has three more. On the beach, a block counts as one of the three touches. After a block touch, the team has only two more contacts. The blocker may take the next contact, though.

Stricter ball-handling

  • No open-hand tips. On the beach you may not push the ball over the net with the fingertips. Soft shots are played with the knuckles ("a cobra") or as a roll shot.
  • Tighter hand-setting. Beach referees judge double contacts on sets much more strictly. Many beach players bump-set instead of hand-setting.
  • Setting over the net. If you hand-set the ball over the net on purpose, you must set it in the direction your shoulders are facing.

No rotation positions, but a serving order

Beach players may stand anywhere on their side when the ball is served; there is no overlap rule. The serving order still alternates between the two partners whenever the team wins the serve back.

How tactics differ

With just two players, the beach game is about deception and reading. Partners use hand signals behind their backs to tell each other which shot to block (line or angle). The defender then covers the part of the court the blocker leaves open. Wind changes everything: serves into the wind float and dive, serves with the wind fly long. Indoor volleyball is faster and more structured, with set plays, quick attacks, and specialist roles that let teams run complex combinations.

Which should you play?

Both. Beach volleyball forces you to become an all-round player and develops ball control, reading and fitness. Indoor volleyball builds power, speed and team systems. Many of the best players in the world have played both, and the skills carry over in both directions.
