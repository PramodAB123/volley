Spike Rally Strategy Guide: Tips to Win More Matches

By the Spike Rally team · Updated 6 November 2026 · 7 min read

Spike Rally hands you the decisions a real volleyball coach and captain make: where to serve, who gets the set, where the spike goes, and how to block. Your players handle the movement. These tips will help you win more of those decisions, against the computer and against friends online.

The four calls

Every rally comes down to four decisions. If you have not played yet, the how to play page covers the controls for mouse, touch and keyboard, and the guided demo in the game walks you through one rally step by step.

  1. Serve: aim anywhere on their court and hit while the power bar swings.
  2. Set: pick which front-row hitter gets the ball, or let the setter decide.
  3. Attack: pick the spot on their court where the spike lands.
  4. Block: commit Left, Middle or Right, or choose Read.

Serving: pressure without free points

  • Aim at the seams. The space between two passers is the weak spot of every formation. A serve there causes more bad passes than one aimed straight at a player.
  • Do not max the power every time. A full-power serve is harder to pass, but it is also less accurate and misses more often. Medium-high power at a seam is a good default.
  • Avoid the lines. A serve aimed at a corner can drift out. Aim half a metre inside.
  • Serve tougher when you are ahead. A missed serve at 20–12 hurts much less than one at 23–23.

Setting: play the blockers, not your favourite hitter

When your team passes, numbers appear over your front-row hitters. Look at where the other team’s blockers are standing before you choose. Sending the set to the side away from the block gives your hitter a bigger gap. If the other team has stacked its blockers on one side, the other side is almost always the right call.

Vary your choices. Against the computer on Hard, and against human opponents, a coach who always sets the same hitter gets read and blocked. Mix it up in the early points and save your best option for the end of the set.

Attacking: open floor beats power

Every spike in Spike Rally is a Power hit, straight at the spot you choose. That means the whole skill is choosing the spot.

  • Look for the empty area between their defenders, usually deep in the middle or in the corner behind the block.
  • Aim away from the block. A spike into stacked blockers comes straight back down on your side.
  • Stay inside the lines. A spot right on a line gives you no margin; a small miss is out. Aim a little inside.
  • Decide early. If you do not pick a spot before the hitter reaches the ball, the hitter swings and misses and the point goes to the other side.

Blocking: when to commit

Read is the safe default: two blockers react to the set, and the block is rarely completely wrong. Commit Left, Middle or Right when you have a reason:

  • The other team has only one strong front-row hitter in this rotation.
  • The other coach has set the same side several times in a row.
  • It is a key point and you would rather gamble on a stuff block than let their best hitter swing freely.

A wrong commit leaves the net open, so do not commit on every rally. Mix commits with Read, just as you mix your sets.

Choosing a team

Each of the eight teams has its own attack, block, pass and serve ratings. A team with strong passing makes your sets more reliable and suits players who like to control the game. A team with strong attack forgives the odd aim that is a little too close to the block. If you are losing a lot on serve receive, try a team with better passing.

Tips for each mode

Quick match

Start with Easy and short sets to learn the calls, then move to Normal. Matches can be 5, 11 or 15 points per set, and one set, best of three or best of five.

Career

Career unlocks one court at a time. Treat each new opponent as a scouting exercise: watch which hitters they use in the first few rallies and adapt your block.

Leagues

Five leagues climb a mountain from the Rookie League to the Legends League. You need a top-two finish to go up. Every match counts, so steady play beats risky serving.

Tournaments

Six knockout cups on a world tour. One bad match ends your run, so play the percentages: Read block by default, serve at the seams, aim inside the lines.

Playing a friend online

Human opponents learn your habits much faster than the computer. Change your setting pattern often, and watch theirs: most players have a favourite hitter, and noticing it is the quickest way to a stuff block.

Stars, the shop and daily rewards

You earn a star for every win, three more for a league title and five more for a tournament cup, and the daily rewards calendar adds more for coming back, including new jerseys for your team. Stars buy new balls and courts in the shop. All of it is cosmetic: everybody plays with the same rules, so skill decides matches.
